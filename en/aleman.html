Learning the language: German, French or Italian

The single factor that most accelerates your integration and career — and the most underestimated before arrival.

Which language to learn based on where you're moving

  • German: German-speaking Switzerland (~62% of the country) — Zurich, Bern, Basel, Zug, Lucerne...
  • French: French-speaking Switzerland — Geneva, Vaud, Neuchâtel, Jura, and part of Fribourg and Valais.
  • Italian: Ticino and part of Graubünden.

An important nuance: Hochdeutsch vs. Schwiizerdütsch

In German-speaking Switzerland, daily life is mostly conducted in Swiss German (Schwiizerdütsch), a set of dialects quite different from standard German with no fixed written form. The good news: everything written, education, and almost the entire formal work environment use Standard German (Hochdeutsch) — that's what you should study first. Dialect comprehension comes naturally over time, just by listening.

Recommended resources

  • Migros Klubschule: the most widespread language school network in Switzerland, level-based in-person courses nationwide.
  • Volkshochschule / Université Populaire: affordable municipal courses, a good option for local integration.
  • Cantonal integration courses: some cantons subsidize language courses for B/L permit holders — ask at your Contrôle des habitants when you register.
  • Before you move: italki, Lingoda, or classes with a native tutor online to arrive with a base (even A2 is a huge leap compared to starting from zero).
  • Apps (Duolingo, Babbel, Busuu): useful as a daily supplement, insufficient on their own to reach a real conversational level.

What level to aim for depending on your goal

  • A2–B1: a reasonable minimum for paperwork, daily life, and dealing with neighbors/administration.
  • B2: opens the door to far more jobs outside fully international companies.
  • C1: needed for public-facing roles, managing local teams, or the public sector.

Many large companies in Zurich, Basel or Zug operate internally in English, especially in tech, pharma and finance — but outside the office, the local language still makes a real difference to your day-to-day life and social integration.

Language by region

  • German — Zurich, Bern, Basel, Zug
  • French — Geneva, Vaud, Valais
  • Italian — Ticino
