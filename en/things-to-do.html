Things to do in Switzerland

A country lived across two completely different seasons. Here are the best activities for each, where to find them, and what to expect.

Summer · June – September

Summer in Switzerland

Once the snow melts, the Alps turn into an endless playground of trails, turquoise lakes, and rock walls. Mild temperatures (18–28°C in the valleys) make it the most popular season to explore outdoors.

🥾

Hiking

Switzerland has over 65,000 km of marked trails, from family strolls to multi-day treks across the Alps.

Best in: Graubünden, Valais, Bern
🧗

Climbing

From beginner-friendly via ferratas to classic Alpine walls. Many local schools offer introductory courses.

Best in: Valais, Grindelwald
🏊

Swimming in alpine lakes

Lake Lucerne, Lake Sils, or Lake Lucerne reach pleasant temperatures in high summer. Many spots have free public access.

Best in: Ticino, Graubünden, Lucerne
🚴

Mountain biking

Trails for every level, with many resorts offering bike lifts so you only pedal on the way down.

Best in: Verbier, Davos, Lenzerheide
🪢

Via ferrata

A middle ground between hiking and climbing: routes equipped with cables and metal rungs on vertical walls, with spectacular views.

Best in: Graubünden, Ticino
🪂

Paragliding

Tandem flights over Alpine valleys are one of the most memorable experiences in the country — Interlaken is the unofficial paragliding capital of Switzerland.

Best in: Interlaken, Verbier

Winter · December – March

Winter in Switzerland

The season Switzerland is world-famous for. Over 200 ski resorts, postcard villages, and a mountain culture that revolves entirely around the snow.

⛷️

Alpine skiing

World-class resorts like Zermatt, St. Moritz, or Verbier, with slopes for every level and glacier skiing available nearly year-round.

Best in: Zermatt, Verbier, St. Moritz
🏂

Snowboarding

Many resorts have dedicated snowparks with jumps and rails, plus off-piste freeride terrain for advanced riders.

Best in: Laax, Davos
🥾

Snowshoeing

The most accessible way to explore the winter landscape with no prior experience needed — dedicated marked trails in most resorts.

Best in: Engadin, Jura
⛸️

Ice skating

Natural rinks on frozen lakes and artificial rinks in almost every mountain village — a classic family activity.

Best in: St. Moritz, Davos
🛷

Sledding (luge)

Natural sled runs stretching several kilometers, some lit up at night — one of the most popular activities for the whole family.

Best in: Grindelwald, Preda-Bergün
🧀

Fondue in a mountain hut

Not a sport, but as Swiss as skiing itself: ending the day in a wooden hut with cheese fondue and mulled wine.

Best in: any ski resort
