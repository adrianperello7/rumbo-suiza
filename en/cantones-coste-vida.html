Cantons & cost of living

Switzerland isn't a single market: choosing a canton affects your language, your taxes, your rent, and your industry.

How to read this table

"Relative taxation" compares combined federal + cantonal + municipal tax against the country average — it's not an exact figure since it also depends on your specific municipality, marital status and income. "Cost of living" combines average rent, health insurance premiums and daily expenses.

Zurich and Zug: highest pay, highest cost

They offer the highest salaries in the country, especially in tech and finance, but also the toughest rent and housing competition. A good choice if your sector pays well and you're prioritizing professional opportunities.

Geneva and Vaud: international, expensive, in French

Ideal if you work for international organizations, NGOs, or private banking. Cost of living is among the highest in Europe, but salaries in those sectors tend to compensate for it.

Ticino: the most "Mediterranean"

Italian language, a milder climate, noticeably lower cost of living — but also lower salaries and a smaller job market. Many workers choose it more for quality of life than salary maximization.

Bern and Valais: balance

A good middle ground between cost of living and opportunities, especially if you work in healthcare, public administration, or tourism.
